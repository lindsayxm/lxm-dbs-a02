This is a ticket selling app for indie performers. Existing ticketing platforms are notoriously high priced and annoyingly complicated.

The goal is to create a simple interface where users can create tickets for their shows/events that:
  - Are held in private, underground, or smaller venues
  - Offers a platform without undue charges or unwarrented fees 
  - Doesn't require joining something or being a member/paying for subscription 
  - Is not a complicated mess

The output/product is a unique link and checkout page for their event, which they can post on their existing social media, streaming service, or website. 
The performer could also create print material such as event posters with QR codes that link to the checkout page. 

When a potential show goer clicks the link posted by the performer, they land on a page with a basic and simple checkout. 
After successful checkout the ticket they purchased is emailed to them. They are responsible for keeping track of the ticket as their is no login - just like irl.

---- **D E S I G N** ----

I chose the final Design System "Storyteller", as I felt it had a modern edge but still contained notes or warmth, luxury, and callback to vintage aesthetic; 
from a time when form mattered as much as function. 

A particularly interesting aspect was how the imprinted gold leaf on a book cover might translate onto the ticket design. 
I attempted to recreate that effect on the ticket itself, giving it a more "real" look, using paper texture overlays, and adding emboss to both text and image. 
I also implemented an image upload cell on the event creation section. 
This allows the performer to add their own embossed logo or image to the ticket they are selling - a personalization touch which is unique and memorable. 

In addition the following criteria needed to be met: 
  **Visual hierarchy**: it's clear what to look at first.
  **Type**: a few sizes and weights, used the same way throughout, and text that's comfortable to read.
  **Color**: a small palette where each color has a purpose.
  **Accessibility**: text contrast is legible.
  Layout: elements are aligned intentionally, related elements are grouped, and it works on desktop
  and mobile.
  **Affordances**: interactions are obvious.
  **States**: controls respond when you hover, focus, or press them.
  **Visual style**: it looks like the style you picked, not the agent's default.
  **Simplicity**: nothing extra. If a word or an image doesn't help, remove it.
